# Task 4: Real-world Data Project (Health Domain) 🏥

## Overview
This repository contains my submission for Task 4 of the data science internship. I chose the **Health domain (Patient Records)** to perform an end-to-end data analysis on the Pima Indians Diabetes dataset. The goal was to clean real-world medical data and identify which physiological features are the strongest predictors of diabetes.

*Context: Applied learning project completed alongside my engineering curriculum at TSSM BSCOER (SPPU 2024 pattern).*

## Data Cleaning & Applied Logic
Real-world data is rarely perfect. During the initial inspection, I found that several patients had a `BloodPressure`, `Glucose`, and `BMI` of exactly `0`. Medically, this is impossible and indicates missing data rather than a true zero. 
- **Solution:** I replaced these impossible zeroes with `NaN` and imputed them using the median of their respective columns. This prevented the machine learning/analytical models from being skewed by artificial drops in the data.

## Key Findings & Conclusions
Based on the `health_insights_dashboard.png` generated by the script, I concluded the following:

1. **Glucose is the Primary Indicator:** The correlation heatmap shows that `Glucose` has the highest positive correlation (0.49) with a positive diabetes outcome. The boxplot clearly visualizes that diabetic patients consistently have significantly higher median glucose levels than healthy patients.
2. **BMI and Age act as Compound Risks:** The scatter plot mapping Age vs. BMI shows a dense cluster of positive diagnoses (Diabetic) among patients over the age of 30 who also possess a BMI greater than 32.
3. **Pregnancy Correlation:** There is a moderate positive correlation (0.22) between the number of pregnancies and the likelihood of developing diabetes later in life.
